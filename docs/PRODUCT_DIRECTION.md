# Where Klierly is going

Klierly starts with a student problem: course material is scattered, study time is limited, and generic AI answers are difficult to trust. The product aims to connect permitted course sources, a realistic daily plan, and practice whose references can be inspected.

## What exists today

A hosted preview and a self-hostable application; document import and search; reviewable course suggestions; study planning; question banks, drill, timed practice and progress; optional provider-backed AI; a course-import extension; approved passkey accounts and administration. The sample course works without a paid model.

This is an early preview. It is not institutionally endorsed, a grading system, or a verified predictor of exam performance. Billing and paid subscriptions are not implemented.

## A possible business

The aim is to make the project sustainable, if student demand supports it. The most natural paid product is convenience: managed hosting, dependable backups and updates, and an explicit AI allowance. A bring-your-own-key option may suit users who want control of their provider spend. Institutional or instructor features are longer-term possibilities that require discovery and appropriate agreements.

These are options to evaluate, not announced plans or pricing. There is no lifetime-access offer, unlimited-AI promise, or guarantee of a permanently free hosted tier. Payment processing, cancellation, refunds, usage limits and support expectations need to be settled before selling subscriptions.

## What to validate next

1. Pilot with a small group using material they are allowed to import. Find out whether the plan and source references improve the study workflow.
2. Evaluate real-model question quality and per-student inference and hosting costs. Structural checks are useful but do not establish teaching quality.
3. Improve passkey/browser compatibility, onboarding, accessibility and restore testing.
4. Confirm the appropriate rights, privacy terms and institutional rules before broader paid access.
5. Test willingness to pay for hosting and a bounded AI allowance, then choose pricing and licensing deliberately.
